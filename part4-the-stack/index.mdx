# Part IV: What the Stack Requires

Suppose your strategy agent needs a pharmaceutical analysis it can't do itself. It hires a research firm's agent, and that agent has to run on your client's own GPU cluster, because the clinical trial data can't leave the building. Three organizations, three billing systems, one answer. Before that answer goes in front of the client, you need to know who did the work, what it cost, where the data went and which rules were checked.

If the pattern holds and the agent economy has to produce an accountability layer, this is the work that layer must do. This part describes it as seven responsibilities, not one protocol: accountability, compute, exchange, trust, enforcement, agency and marketplace. They share interfaces, but they are not a ladder where each layer waits on the one below. Together they cover tracking costs, tracing conclusions, governing data, allocating resources and settling transactions across organizational boundaries.

The stack rests on a foundation: sovereignty. The accountability guarantees mean nothing if the infrastructure is controlled by a third party. And the stack must be an open protocol, not a proprietary platform, because the point is interoperability across every boundary.
