title: "Part I: The World Has Changed"
---

Suppose you are a junior lawyer with a stack of contracts. Each one takes you about four hours: read it, redline it, summarize what the client is signing up for. A language model now does that first pass in about 90 seconds, and a senior lawyer reviews its output in ten minutes. Your four billable hours just became ten minutes of someone else's oversight.

That is why this shift is different. The internet collapsed distribution costs. Cloud computing made infrastructure variable. Mobile put a computer in every pocket. Each of those shifts spawned new monopolies, new opportunities and new dependencies. Each one changed where work happens or what infrastructure costs.

AI changes who does the work. Tasks that used to need human specialists (contract review, data analysis, clinical documentation, code generation) are now done by software, in production, at scale. Real professionals depend on the output. AI has moved from tool to worker.

The ecosystem enabling this transition is already vast and accelerating. Developers build entire applications through conversation. Multi-agent frameworks coordinate specialized AI workers across organizational boundaries. Interoperability protocols backed by hundreds of organizations are establishing the communication standards for an agent economy. The infrastructure for AI-as-worker is being assembled right now, in the open, by thousands of independent teams who may not realize they're building the same thing.
