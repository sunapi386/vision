The Triangle Quest

Every game you play is millions of triangles and little dots of light, sixty times a second. This quest ends where you can build the machine that draws them. It starts tonight, with one triangle.

In 3D graphics, once you can draw a triangle, you can do anything. Alyssa Rosenzweig, who reverse-engineered the Apple M1 GPU and wrote its driver. She drew her first triangle a few weeks in. She started in high school.

Draw one. Right now.

Drag the three corners. Each corner is just an address on the grid: how far across, how far down. Three addresses make a triangle. That is the smallest flat shape there is, and it is what your whole game world is built from.

Try: drag a corner to make it point down like a DRG drill. Hit Launch it and it slides across the screen, the way every moving thing in every game is really just a triangle whose numbers change each frame. Turn on Aim mode and the top corner tracks your mouse like a reticle.

Why a triangle?

Because three points is the smallest, simplest flat piece a computer can shade, and any shape at all can be built out of them. Master Chief's helmet. A whole Deep Rock cave. It is all triangles, stitched together and colored in by a chip called the GPU, whose one job is turning math into which lights are on.

You did not memorize that. You just dragged it. That is the whole game: figure the thing out and build it, instead of being told to remember it. Alyssa figured out a GPU that came with no instructions at all. So can you. We have ten years and a couple of real graphics cards in the house.

Where you train between ranks

The quest is the map. The training ground is the dad-math lab: dozens of short, playable lessons that each take apart one gear inside the games you already play. Newest is the AI Lab, written from a walk we took, on what an agent really is and how a machine learns by guessing, measuring, and adjusting. Every "Train here" link above drops you straight into one.

AI is going to write ordinary code better than anyone by the time you are 14. So we are not building a coder. We are building the person who can point a swarm of AIs at a machine no one understands, tell whether the answer is actually right, and stay on it for years. That person wins.
