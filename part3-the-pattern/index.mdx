# Part III: The Pattern

By 1993, millions of people could browse the web, send email and transfer files. Almost nobody would type a credit card number into a browser, because everything crossed the network in cleartext. The network worked. Nobody trusted it with money. Then, in 1995, Netscape shipped SSL, and an internet economy became possible.

That is the pattern this part follows. In four cases (commerce, the internet, international banking, containerized software), a shared way to record or verify activity grew alongside a new capability. The timing and institutions were different in each case. What survived was often a common method, even as particular tools disappeared.

Agents already spend money, pass work to other agents and produce conclusions that inform consequential decisions. There is no shared record that follows all of that work across organizations. The historical cases suggest one might emerge, but they cannot tell us what form it will take, who will control it or whose interests it will serve.
